\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Detecting lateral movement as anomalous-edge detection over a temporal graph is
settled ground. We have argued that the substrate is not: a Zero-Trust policy decision
point observes a five-node causal chain per request, and collapsing it to a
host-to-host authentication edge discards the one signal that makes credential reuse
from an unfamiliar client expressible at all. Modelling the client configuration as a
node, rather than as a feature, is what turns that class from undetectable into
detectable.

Two further positions we would defend independently of our numbers. First, a streaming
detector must not be the authority on what enters its own baseline; the commit gate
belongs to the policy engine, and results should be reported under that gate rather
than under the model's own verdict. Second, a synthetic benchmark deserves a permanent
leakage audit shipped alongside it. Ours nearly invalidated our own results --- a
single mislabelled column reached AUC \FloorLatPre{} on lateral movement by itself ---
and after the fix the same class sits at \FloorLatPost, meaning the task became
genuinely harder, not merely more honest. We would rather publish that number than the
one it replaced.

\ifpreliminary
The immediate work is regeneration: every quantitative claim in this draft predates the
de-leakage and must be reproduced on the corrected generator before any of it is relied
upon. That includes the per-component ablations, which are withdrawn rather than
softened, and the seed grid needed to bring the reported dispersion in line with the
run-to-run variance of Section~\ref{sec:limits}.
\else
Beyond the results reported here, the open work is threefold.
\fi

Beyond that, three directions. A second, independently constructed synthetic stream
tests whether the conclusions survive a generator we did not write --- the sharpest
available check against results that are properties of our own simulator. The AIT
corpus offers the scaled full-chain evaluation, with eight independent testbeds
amounting to a cross-testbed generalisation study and real JA3 recoverable from packet
captures. And running LANL as the explicit ``configuration node removed'' arm, under
the split conventions the literature has settled on, would make the degradation legible
in the terms the community already reads --- turning the one corpus on which our
architecture cannot be shown at its best into the cleanest available demonstration of
why its distinguishing component is there.
