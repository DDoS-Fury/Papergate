\section{Threat Model and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Setting}

A PDP receives a stream of access requests $e_1, e_2, \dots$ in arrival order. Request
$e_i$ at time $t_i$ carries a client network origin $s_i$, a TLS configuration
fingerprint $c_i$, a device identity $d_i$, an authenticated principal $u_i$, a
requested resource $r_i$, and a feature vector $m_i \in \mathbb{R}^{10}$ (request
method, sensor state, TLS-trust bit, request volume, inter-arrival gap). The detector
must emit a score $a_i \in [0,1]$ using only $e_1, \dots, e_i$ --- no future events, no
batch. This is not a modelling preference: at decision time the future does not exist.

Two consequences are enforced throughout. Response-side fields are excluded from
$m_i$, because an HTTP status or a response size is not available when the decision is
made; we removed both after finding they acted as near-deterministic label channels.
And the evaluation replays the test stream event by event, never scoring an event
against a memory that has already ingested it.

\subsection{Attacker}

The adversary has obtained valid credentials --- by phishing, by dumping them from a
compromised host, or by session theft --- and issues requests that the policy engine
\emph{permits}. They may run their own tooling, from their own machine, on their own
network. They may probe before moving. They are assumed \emph{not} to have cloned the
victim's TLS stack, device attestation and access habits simultaneously; an adversary
who has done all three is out of scope, and we say so in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

We target four classes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Policy violation} --- role, clearance or tier insufficient. Owned by
  the policy engine, which blocks it upstream. Reported for completeness; it is not a
  contribution of the model.
  \item \textbf{Contextual} --- broken TLS trust, IDS alerts. Largely caught by rules
  (a rule baseline takes roughly 97\% of it). Also not where the value is.
  \item \textbf{Lateral movement} --- authorized but non-habitual. Feature-identical to
  benign traffic by construction. \emph{The genuine target.}
  \item \textbf{Credential reuse} --- a known principal's credentials arriving from a
  client configuration that principal has never used. Policy-clean and signal-clean;
  visible only on the \textit{config}\,$\rightarrow$\,\textit{user} binding.
\end{itemize}

The last two are what the architecture is for, and they are the two for which the
leakage audit of Section~\ref{sec:setup} permits no exemptions.

\subsection{Learning regime}

Training is \textbf{one-class / semi-supervised}, not unsupervised, and we are precise
about this because the distinction is routinely blurred. Labels are used in exactly two
places: to select the training set (benign events only) and to calibrate the operating
threshold on a validation slice. No label ever enters the computation of a test score.
The primary threshold is additionally \emph{label-free}: a quantile of the
signal-clean benign score distribution at a target false-positive rate. A
cost-sensitive threshold, which does require labelled lateral events in the validation
window, is reported separately and only as a declared upper bound.

\label{fig:gate}

A streaming detector with a learned baseline faces a circularity: to stay current it
must absorb new traffic, and if it absorbs traffic it itself judged benign, a patient
adversary can walk the baseline to wherever they like. The usual framing treats this as
an accepted risk. We treat it as a protocol requirement (Fig.~\ref{fig:gate}): the
memory and the temporal neighbour store advance \textbf{only} on events the external
policy engine admits.

The distinction between the model's own threshold and the commit gate is not cosmetic.
A model that decides what to remember drifts, and the false-positive rate runs away. All
figures in this paper are produced under the external gate. A self-gated mode exists in
the implementation, for deployments without a policy engine in the loop, and it is
\emph{not} the protocol under which anything here was measured.

Two residual exposures follow directly and are not solved: an adversary whose events
the policy engine admits still poisons the baseline, and a benign entity persistently
scored anomalous is never learned (starvation). Both are pushed to the orchestrator ---
the engine is the decision authority, and a short grace period for newly admitted
entities mitigates the second.
