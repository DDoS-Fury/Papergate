\section{External Validity}
\label{sec:external}

\subsection{A negative result about the available corpora}

We surveyed the public datasets that could plausibly host a five-node ZTA chain and
report two findings that we believe are useful independently of this system.

\textbf{No public dataset is built for Zero Trust or continuous authorization.} The
ZTA literature on trust scoring and policy decision points is evaluated almost
entirely on bespoke, unreleased simulations. There is no shared benchmark, and that is
why our primary evaluation is synthetic.

\textbf{No public dataset delivers user identity and TLS client fingerprints together
as given fields.} This splits the candidate space cleanly. Authentication-graph
corpora --- LANL~\cite{lanl}, DARPA OpTC~\cite{optc}, CERT~\cite{cert} --- are large
and carry lateral-movement ground truth, but have no client fingerprint whatsoever.
Testbed corpora that ship raw packet captures --- AIT~\cite{ait},
CIC-IDS2017~\cite{cicids} --- allow real JA3 to be recomputed, but either lack an
identity layer entirely (CIC-IDS2017 has no user field, which is fatal for a ZTA
framing) or lack a lateral-movement label (AIT labels scanning through exfiltration,
not movement).

One corpus sits in the intersection.

\subsection{PicoDomain}

PicoDomain~\cite{picodomain} is a 16\,MB, \PicoSpan-day capture of a Windows-domain
intrusion across five workstations and a domain controller, released as Zeek JSON logs
with a manually recorded red-team log. It is the only public corpus we found in which
all five nodes are backed by a real field, and the reason is its
\texttt{ssl.log}: \PicoSSLrecords{} records, \emph{all} of them carrying a
\texttt{ja3} column, over \PicoJAthree{} distinct fingerprints. Its
\texttt{kerberos.log} \texttt{client} field simultaneously yields human principals and
machine accounts, i.e.\ user and device identity from one field.

The red-team log covers the full chain we care about: a trojanised archive, privilege
escalation, \emph{Mimikatz} credential dumping, domain enumeration, then remote
execution on three further hosts via DCOM and WMI, and finally a Meterpreter session
under a different account. Lateral movement and credential reuse are both present.

\subsection{What is observed and what is reconstructed}

We are explicit about the weakest joint, because it is the one a reviewer should press
on. Zeek's \texttt{uid} identifies a \emph{connection}, and the overlap between the
\texttt{uid} sets of \texttt{ssl} and \texttt{kerberos}/\texttt{ntlm} is exactly
\PicoUidOverlap: the TLS handshake and the authentication happen on different
connections. \textbf{The \textit{config}\,$\rightarrow$\,\textit{user} binding --- the
edge that carries our credential-reuse class --- is therefore not observed in this
dataset. It is reconstructed}, per source address, as the last identity and last
fingerprint seen within a time-to-live.

The time-to-live is set to \PicoTTL~s, the default Kerberos ticket lifetime, i.e.\ the
window over which the domain itself treats a logon session as valid. It is chosen for
that reason rather than tuned against a metric, but coverage depends on it and the
dependence belongs in any result reported on this corpus: user-binding coverage is
\PicoCovUserShortest{} at 900\,s, \PicoCovUserShort{} at 3600\,s and \PicoCovUser{} at
\PicoTTL~s, with device and configuration coverage at \PicoCovDevice{} and
\PicoCovConfig{} respectively. Events that cannot be attributed fall back to
\emph{per-address} sentinel nodes rather than a shared ``unknown'' node, so an
unattributed event never inherits another entity's memory.

The resulting stream carries \PicoEvents{} access events over \PicoNodes{} nodes
(\PicoUsers{} users, \PicoDevices{} devices, \PicoSources{} sources, \PicoConfigs{}
configurations, \PicoResources{} resources), with \PicoNlateral{} lateral,
\PicoNtheft{} credential-reuse and \PicoNctx{} contextual events --- an anomalous
fraction of \PicoAnomFrac. Labels are derived by matching each access event against a
red-team row within $\pm90$\,s on host or principal, with the action text mapped to our
taxonomy by a published regular-expression table. The authors state the red-team
timestamps are accurate to $\pm1$ minute; this is an approximate labelling of a
hand-written log, and must be read as one.

At eight source addresses and \PicoSpan{} days, PicoDomain is a \textbf{case study, not
a headline benchmark}. Variance over \PicoNlateral{} lateral events is high, and the
policy class is not evaluable at all, since the corpus carries no roles, clearances or
asset classification. Its value is qualitative and specific: it is existence proof that
the five-node chain is observable in real enterprise telemetry, and the only public
setting where the configuration node can be exercised on real fingerprints.

\subsection{Model results on PicoDomain}
\label{sec:pico-model}

We ran the same one-class training, calibration and streaming-evaluation pipeline used
on the synthetic stream (\texttt{tests/eval\_picodomain.py}) directly on the mapped
PicoDomain stream (\PicoModelSettings), with no per-corpus hyperparameter tuning.
Ranking quality is clearly above chance on all three classes: aggregate
AUC \PicoModelAggAuc{} / AP \PicoModelAggAp; lateral AUC \PicoModelLatAuc{} / AP
\PicoModelLatAp{} ($n=\PicoNlateral$); credential-theft AUC \PicoModelTheftAuc{} / AP
\PicoModelTheftAp{} ($n=\PicoNtheft$); contextual AUC \PicoModelCtxAuc{} / AP
\PicoModelCtxAp{} ($n=\PicoModelCtxN$, the test-split share of \PicoNctx{} total). This
is markedly weaker than the synthetic result,
as expected from a corpus two orders of magnitude smaller and with only eight source
addresses, and we report it as a case-study signal, not a competing headline number.

We do not report an operating recall on this corpus, and the omission is deliberate
rather than an oversight. PicoDomain's red-team campaign is concentrated in the final
portion of the capture --- the first lateral and credential-theft events land at 84.3\%
and 84.2\% of the stream by index --- so under the chronological 70/10/20 split the
training window is entirely benign (the correct condition for one-class training) but
the validation window used to calibrate the detection threshold contains zero lateral
and zero theft events. A threshold fit against zero positive examples is uninformative
by construction, and reporting a recall number derived from it would misattribute a
split artifact to the model. We regard this as a genuine limitation of evaluating an
operating point on a single, tail-concentrated intrusion campaign, not of the ranking
itself, and leave a split that also gives a representative validation sample of the
attack (e.g.\ time-stratified rather than count-stratified) to future work on a larger
real-world corpus.

\subsection{LANL as an ablation, not a peer benchmark}

Omitting LANL from a lateral-movement paper invites an obvious question, so we state
our position. On LANL the source and device nodes both collapse onto the computer
identifier; the internal/external flag becomes constant; the configuration node
degenerates to the (authentication type, logon type) pair, which is low-cardinality and
heavily null; the resource is host-granular with no sensitivity metadata; and every
static attribute must be synthesised. \textbf{The credential-reuse class --- the reason
the configuration node exists --- is not evaluable on LANL at all.}

The honest use of the corpus is therefore as the ``configuration node removed'' arm of
our own architecture, run under the split conventions established in the
literature~\cite{gfm} so the degradation is legible. That is future work, and the
harness for it exists. What we do not do is present a LANL number beside Euler's and
Argus's as though it measured the same system: it would measure our architecture with
its distinguishing component disabled.

\subsection{Next}

The scaled full-chain evaluation belongs on the AIT Log Data Set~\cite{ait}: eight
independent testbeds, real client addresses with a real RFC1918 boundary, real
principals across SSH, webmail and audit logs, real request URIs, and raw packet
captures from which genuine JA3 fingerprints can be recomputed. Eight independent
chronological streams amount to a cross-testbed generalisation study for a streaming
inductive model --- an experimental design none of the snapshot-based systems can
offer. Its gap is the lateral-movement label, which an attack-only corpus from the same
testbed lineage could supply by overlay.
