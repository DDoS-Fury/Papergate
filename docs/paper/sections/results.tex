Table~\ref{tab:qualitative} states the position. The four reference systems share a
substrate --- a host-to-host authentication log --- and consequently share a ceiling:
none of them can represent a familiar principal arriving under an unfamiliar client
configuration, because there is no node for the configuration. They also share a
benchmark, which makes them mutually comparable and makes us not comparable with them:
LANL carries no client fingerprint, so running on it would mean evaluating our
architecture with its distinguishing component disabled. We do exactly that in
Section~\ref{sec:external}, and treat it as an ablation rather than as a comparison.

Two further axes are worth naming. The snapshot-based systems discretise time into
windows; a PDP cannot wait for a window to close. And none of the four reports results
under a commit gate held by an external decision authority --- the question of what
enters the baseline does not arise when the model is evaluated offline over a fixed
graph, but it is the first question in deployment.

The comparison that carries the argument is the static GNN
(Table~\ref{tab:baselines}). It has the same graph structure, the same causal history
counters and the same precursor prior; it lacks only the recurrent memory and the
temporal neighbourhood. On lateral movement it sits at chance (\LatAucGNN), while the
TGN reaches \LatAucTGN. The lateral signal therefore lives in the temporal machinery,
not in the counters --- which every baseline has. Its apparent recall (\LatRecGNN) is
an artefact of a permissive threshold over a chance-level ranking and should not be
read as detection.

The tabular one-class detectors behave as expected: they do well on the classes that a
rule engine also handles and poorly on lateral movement, which by construction has no
tabular signature. Supervised XGBoost, which sees the labels, is close to the TGN on
ranking (\LatAucXGB{} vs \LatAucTGN) and well ahead on operating recall
(\LatRecXGB{} vs \LatRecTGN) --- the gap that labels buy, and the honest measure of
what the one-class regime costs.

Interpreting these numbers requires perspective on the broader literature. Earlier
works on static graph snapshots frequently claimed near-perfect ranking ($>0.98$ AUC),
yet recent reproducibility studies on imbalanced enterprise logs show that
unsupervised Average Precision on sparse lateral activity routinely drops below $0.10$.
On our de-leaked stream, where lateral movement is strictly bounded by a single-feature
floor of $\FloorLatPost$, reaching \LatAucTGN{} with an AP of \LatApTGN{} under
benign-only one-class training represents genuine relational learning rather than
synthetic dataset shortcuts.

Table~\ref{tab:panelb} isolates the configuration node by ablating it on the same
stream. The introduction of the configuration node yields a measurable improvement across
both ranking and detection metrics: aggregate AUC improves by \BaggAucD{} (to \BaggAucIV),
while lateral-movement AUC increases by \BlatAucD{} (from \BlatAucIII{} to \BlatAucIV)
along with a marked gain in lateral Average Precision (\BlatApD). Operating recall on lateral
movement improves substantially by \BlatRecD{} (reaching \BlatRecIV), while maintaining a
low benign false-positive rate (\BfprIV). On a theft-rich stream, credential-reuse
detection with the configuration node reaches AUC \TheftAucOn{} and recall
\TheftRecOn{} ($n=\TheftN$ theft events); ablating the node drops both to AUC
\TheftAucOff{} and recall \TheftRecOff{}, a cost of \TheftRecallDelta{} recall on
credential reuse and \TheftLateralDelta{} on lateral movement on the same stream
(\TheftSettings).

The stronger argument for the node is not the delta but the expressiveness: on a
four-node chain the credential-reuse class has no edge on which to be anomalous. The
measured recall gain is evidence that the edge is used; the architectural point is that
without it the class is not merely harder, it is unrepresentable.

\subsection{What we do not claim}

Per-component ablation deltas --- for the hashed identity, the history counters, the
precursor prior and the structural head --- are \textbf{withdrawn}. Two mutually
contradictory series existed in our own records for the same quantities, neither backed
by a run log, and the hypothesis that explained the dominance of the history features
in the older series was a $\Delta t$ shortcut that has since been removed. They will be
regenerated as per-seed paired deltas with a signed-rank test and bootstrap intervals.
Until then, no claim about the contribution of an individual component is supported,
and we make none.
