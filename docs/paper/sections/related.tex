\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Lateral movement as anomalous-edge detection}

Casting lateral movement as anomalous-edge detection over a temporal graph of
authentication events is an established formulation, and we state up front that it is
not ours. \textbf{Euler}~\cite{euler} composes a graph neural network with a recurrent
encoder over discrete snapshots of a host-to-host authentication graph and detects
red-team activity on LANL~\cite{lanl}; it is the canonical reference for the task.
\textbf{Argus}~\cite{argus} reports comparable performance on the same corpus and is
treated as the incumbent state of the art in later work. \textbf{LMDetect}~\cite{lmdetect}
classifies time-aware subgraphs extracted around each authentication event over a
heterogeneous multigraph. A recent \textbf{graph foundation model}~\cite{gfm} pretrains
on host-to-host structure and reports results on both LANL and DARPA OpTC with fixed
split conventions --- first 41 hours for training on LANL, six days for training and
three for evaluation on OpTC --- that have become a comparability standard.

Every one of these systems takes an authentication log as its substrate: nodes are
hosts (sometimes hosts and users), edges are logon events. That choice fixes what is
detectable. It also explains why they are directly comparable with each other and why
we are not directly comparable with them: our numbers are produced on a substrate that
carries a client-configuration node those graphs do not have, and their benchmark
corpus does not contain the field that would populate it. We return to this in
Section~\ref{sec:external} and treat LANL as an ablation of our own architecture ---
the ``config node removed'' arm --- rather than as a shared benchmark.

\subsection{Temporal graph learning}

We build directly on the Temporal Graph Network of Rossi et al.~\cite{tgn}, which
maintains a recurrent per-node memory updated by event messages and computes
embeddings by attending over recent temporal neighbours. The relevant distinction
against the systems above is the granularity of time. Euler and the snapshot-based
family discretise the stream into fixed windows and encode the sequence of snapshots;
a TGN consumes events continuously and updates only the entities each event touches.
For a PDP that must answer per request, the continuous formulation is not merely more
elegant, it is the deployable one: there is no window to wait for. Related continuous
formulations include TGAT~\cite{tgat} and JODIE~\cite{jodie}. TGNs have subsequently
been applied to anomaly detection over dynamic and provenance graphs, though not, to
our knowledge, over a ZTA request stream with an explicit client-configuration node.

\subsection{Zero Trust and policy-driven authorization}

NIST SP 800-207~\cite{nist800207} separates the policy decision point from the policy
enforcement point and mandates continuous evaluation. In practice the PDP is a policy
engine such as Open Policy Agent~\cite{opa} evaluating declarative rules. A learned
detector in this setting is an \emph{advisor} to that engine, not a replacement for it,
and this has a consequence that is usually left implicit: the detector must not be the
authority on what enters its own baseline. We make that explicit and measure under it
(Section~\ref{sec:problem}). Surveys of the ZTA literature note that trust-score and
PDP proposals are evaluated almost exclusively on bespoke, unreleased simulations ---
a gap we confirm empirically in Section~\ref{sec:external}.

\subsection{Client fingerprinting}

JA3~\cite{ja3} hashes the ordered parameters of a TLS \texttt{ClientHello} into a
fingerprint of the originating library and its configuration. It is widely deployed for
malware-family attribution. We use it differently: not to recognise a known-bad
fingerprint, but as a \emph{node identity} whose habitual association with a principal
is learned, so that a familiar credential arriving under an unfamiliar fingerprint is
anomalous by structure rather than by signature. This makes the mechanism robust to
fingerprints never seen at training time, at the cost of being blind to an adversary who
faithfully clones the victim's TLS stack.

\subsection{Position}

The task is not new; the substrate, the gate and the deployment protocol are where we
claim contribution. Concretely: (i) a five-node ZTA chain including a configuration
node, which makes credential reuse from an unfamiliar client expressible at all;
(ii) an anti-poisoning commit gate driven by an external policy engine, reported as
the protocol under which all figures were produced rather than as a design note; and
(iii) an inductive streaming deployment with hashed node identity and verified
train/serve equivalence. We report where each of these is supported by measurement and
where it is not.
